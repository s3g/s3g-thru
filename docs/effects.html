Post-engine sound

Effects

Every playback engine feeds one clip-local stereo chain: tone shaping, drive, bit and rate reduction, cross-feedback delay, stereo cross-pan, rotary speaker, then tape. Three shared pages follow Mix / Rec for every engine.

The figures below use Lanes, where these are pages 7–9 of 13. Their order is the same for every engine, but the displayed numbers change because each engine has a different number of contextual pages before Mix / Rec.

Input Monitor stays dry, which prevents the effects from becoming a microphone monitoring loop. Browser Preview and Resample use the complete processed chain.

The final safety limiter smooths fast peaks from overlapping notes and live parameter changes with 1.5 ms of lookahead. Both channels share the same gain, preserving their stereo balance.

Effects — every parameter

Effects

KnobDisplayRangeWhat it controls
1TONE−1.00–1.00Darkens toward −1 with a low-pass contour; brightens toward +1 by progressively removing low frequencies. Zero is bypass.
2DRIV0.00–1.00Adds level-dependent soft saturation before reduction and delay. Its smoothed, continuous transfer does not generate isolated clicks, although added level and harmonics can make an existing edge more prominent.
3BITS0.00–1.00Moves from transparent resolution toward coarse four-bit quantization. Quantizer edges are deliberately digital; use 0.00 when clean playback is required.
4RATE1–16Holds each stereo sample for this many output frames. Values above 1 deliberately create stepped high-frequency edges; use 1 for full-rate clean playback.
5DLY1–750 msFree-running delay time in milliseconds. Live and recorded changes use uninterrupted tap crossfades; a new value arriving mid-transition is queued rather than jumping the active read head.
6FDBK0–90%Amount returned to the delay. The feedback path is safely limited but high values can still build a dense tail.
7WDTH0–100%At 0%, each delay channel feeds itself. At 100%, left and right cross-feed for ping-pong motion.
8MIX0–100%Delay wet balance. Tone, Drive, Bits, and Rate remain audible at 0% because they precede the dry/wet delay stage.

Continuous effect controls glide during live moves and recorded automation. Ordinary tone, drive, feedback, stereo, rotary, and tape changes use a short 5 ms response; wet/dry controls use a longer 12 ms fade because they blend signals with different phases. Delay-time changes use a 10 ms tap crossfade and complete it before moving toward the newest queued value. BITS and RATE remain intentionally stepped because their discontinuities are part of the reduction effect.

Stereo / Rotary

KnobDisplayRangeWhat it controls
1X-PN−100–100Cross-pans the complete stereo image. NORM at −100 preserves left and right, MONO at 0 sends their averaged sum to both outputs, and SWAP at +100 exchanges left and right. Intermediate values progressively collapse or reopen the field.
2RATE0.00–8.00 HzSets the high-horn target speed; the low drum runs slightly slower. At 0.00, both rotors brake to a stop at their current angles rather than resetting their phase.
3DPTH0–100%Controls stereo level motion and Doppler pitch depth for the independently moving high horn and low drum.
4SPRD0–100%Controls only the width of the rotary wet signal. Zero folds the cabinet to mono while retaining its movement; 100% preserves the full modeled stereo field.
5RAMP50 ms–8.00 sSets rotor acceleration and braking inertia. The high horn responds first and the heavier low drum follows more slowly. Encoder spacing is finer below 500 ms and progressively broader for long ramps.
6BALDRUM–EVEN–HORNBalances the low drum against the high horn. The end positions isolate one rotor; EVEN keeps both bands at equal nominal gain.
7XOVR250–2000 HzSets the crossover between the drum and horn. Lower values give more of the sound to the horn; higher values give more to the drum. Encoder steps are deliberately finer in the lower range.
8MIX0–100%Blends the rotary cabinet with the cross-panned signal. Zero bypasses the rotary stage; 100% is the rotating cabinet alone. Its lower-right position matches MIX on the main Effects page.

The rotary stage splits the signal into a low drum and high horn, moves them at related but different rates, and combines stereo amplitude movement with short modulated delays. The high horn accelerates faster than the heavier drum. A reduced amount of the original side signal is retained so unusually wide or phase-opposed stereo material does not disappear. X-PAN is before Rotary, while SPRD acts on the rotary wet signal only, so MONO can deliberately give the speaker a centered source that the cabinet spreads into motion.

Tape

KnobDisplayRangeWhat it controls
1FMT2″, 1″, ½″, ¼″, ⅛″Selects a tape-width character. Wider settings retain more bandwidth and stereo separation with gentler saturation; narrower settings progressively soften the top end, narrow the image, increase the head contour, and compress peaks. Changes morph the modeled head geometry and condition over 20 ms instead of jumping the read position. The default is 2″.
2REC0–100%Sets the record-head level. Higher settings add level-dependent compression and restrained saturation before the tape condition is applied.
3AGE0–100%Moves from fresh tape toward worn media by reducing high-frequency bandwidth and stereo stability, softening transients, strengthening the head contour, and increasing compression. It does not automatically add loud hiss.
4CRNK0–100%Controls the frequency and depth of occasional soft tape-drag events. Each event briefly reduces bandwidth and level with a small, smoothly moving speed sag. It does not generate dust, scratches, clicks, pops, or phonograph-style surface noise. Zero is clean.
5MECH0–100%Combines coherent wow, flutter, and non-periodic transport drift. The macro primarily increases depth, while its composite motion avoids behaving like one synchronized LFO. Zero is stable.
6INRTOFF, 50 ms–4.0 sAdds mechanical deceleration and acceleration to Loop Stack Hold. Higher settings take longer to stop and restart. At rest the tape wet path is silent; releasing Hold returns it with a rising varispeed gesture. OFF leaves Stack Hold unchanged.
7HISSOFF, −83–−54 dBFSSets a filtered stereo tape-noise floor in decibels. The bottom −84 dBFS detent is displayed as OFF and generates no noise. Hiss level remains independent of FMT and AGE; the default −72 dBFS setting is deliberately restrained.
8MIX0–100%Blends the complete tape path with the signal leaving Rotary. Its audio-tapered curve gives finer control over subtle low wet amounts; 0% is a clean bypass.

FMT is a musical character macro rather than a calibration of one particular recorder. The first four choices correspond to familiar professional open-reel tape widths. The ⅛″ label is familiar cassette shorthand; compact cassette tape is actually 0.150″ (3.81 mm) wide. Tape speed, track layout, formulation, and machine alignment also affect real tape fidelity. FMT establishes a physical baseline, so AGE, CRNK, and MECH become somewhat more pronounced on narrower formats.

INRT affects only the Tape wet path. At MIX below 100%, the untreated portion continues while the modeled transport stops. This keeps Stack Hold useful as a subtle gesture at low tape mixes and allows a complete tape stop at 100%.

Resampling: Resample records the processed instrument output, including this chain. Input recording captures the selected Move input instead. Browser Preview also passes through the effects, while Monitor remains dry.

Performance-loop motion

Every control on all three effects pages can be recorded in a performance loop. Begin Replace or Overdub recording, then turn an encoder. Continuous values interpolate between recorded points; stepped choices change at their recorded thresholds while their audio paths retain the appropriate safety transition. Undo Take removes the newest layer of effect motion together with the other notes and gestures in that take.
