Post-engine sound

Effects

Every playback engine feeds one compact stereo chain: tone shaping, drive, bit and rate reduction, then a cross-feedback delay. The page is available after Mix / Rec for every engine, and all eight values belong to the current clip.

Effects — every parameter

KnobDisplayRangeDetail
1TONE−1.00–1.00Darkens toward −1 with a low-pass contour; brightens toward +1 by progressively removing low frequencies. Zero is bypass.
2DRIV0.00–1.00Adds level-dependent soft saturation. It is placed before reduction and delay.
3BITS0.00–1.00Moves from transparent resolution toward coarse four-bit quantization.
4RATE1–16Holds each stereo sample for this many output frames. One is transparent; higher values add stepped high-frequency aliasing.
5DLY1–750 msDelay time in milliseconds. It is deliberately free-running so recorded parameter motion can push it away from the performance clock.
6FDBK0–90%Amount returned to the delay. The feedback path is safely limited but high values can still build a dense tail.
7WDTH0–100%At 0%, each delay channel feeds itself. At 100%, left and right cross-feed for ping-pong motion.
8MIX0–100%Delay wet balance. Tone, Drive, Bits, and Rate remain audible at 0% because they precede the dry/wet delay stage.

Resampling: Resample records the processed instrument output, including this chain. Input recording captures the selected Move input instead. Browser Preview also passes through the effects, while Monitor remains dry.

Performance-loop motion

Every Effects control can be recorded as a continuous performance-loop gesture. Begin Replace or Overdub recording, then turn an Effects encoder. Undo Take removes the newest layer of effect motion together with the other notes and gestures in that take.
