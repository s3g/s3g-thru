Hardware reference

Shortcut reference

The same control can change meaning by view, but recording gestures remain deliberately distinct: Record captures performance data; Shift+Record captures stereo audio.

On-device help

After leaving takeover, hold Shift, touch the volume knob without turning it, and press Step 2 to open Schwung Global Settings. Hold Shift and click the main encoder for its section picker, choose System, then open Help → Modules → s3g Thru for the quick reference. Choose a topic with the main encoder, click to open, and turn to scroll. Back returns to the topic list. The reference covers samples, lanes and engines, instrument modes, performance loops, and safety shortcuts. Inside Thru, Shift + Menu continues to open the diagnostic reports shown below.

Schwung Skipback

Shift + Capture saves Schwung Skipback audio. This shortcut belongs to Schwung and remains available during takeover. A “Skipback saved” message means Schwung saved recent audio from its rolling buffer as a WAV.

FX Sends: in Split, Shared Dual, or Quad, hold Capture and press Track 3 to open Mix/FX, or use Capture + Menu → Mix/FX. Choose FX SENDS when using the hub list.

Reading the System Report

DisplayWhat it reportsHow to read it
RENDER PEAKLongest audio-block render time since takeover started, in microseconds.Lower leaves more time for the rest of the device. Compare it with WORK PEAK on the next report page; rendering is only part of the work needed for playback.
RENDER MISSESNumber of render calls that exceeded one audio-block period.0 is healthy. If it rises while clicks or gaps occur, reduce costly grain/event settings or active polyphony. This count includes time waiting during the render call and excludes control and query calls. Check Render Timing to distinguish CPU cost from waiting.
CPU PEAKLongest CPU time used by the rendering thread for one audio block.Compare it with the roughly 2,902 µs block period. A rising CPU MISSES count indicates that rendering itself consumed more CPU time than one block allows.
WALL PEAKThe same elapsed render-time peak shown as RENDER PEAK.Includes running and waiting time. Each peak is a separate historical maximum; subtracting displayed peaks does not reconstruct one particular block.
WAIT PEAKLargest per-block difference between elapsed render time and rendering-thread CPU time.A rise with CPU MISSES at zero points toward descheduling or blocking during rendering. It does not identify the cause or measure work outside the render call.
CPU MISSESNumber of render calls whose measured CPU time exceeded one audio-block period.Use this to distinguish DSP load from a high elapsed-time peak. CPU fields show N/A when the CPU clock is unavailable or no fresh reading has been taken.
CONTROL PEAKLongest live parameter or MIDI operation, in microseconds.Watch this while changing controls or playing. A rise helps distinguish control work from engine rendering.
QUERY PEAKLongest display/status query, in microseconds.A large value points to work associated with reading or updating instrument information.
FADE BUF MISSESOutgoing pattern audio-buffer underruns during a crossfade.A rising count can explain a gap or dip during a transition: the outgoing performance was not ready in time. This is separate from RENDER MISSES and WORK MISSES. The count is history and can remain after audio recovers. Timed fades and DONE keep advancing through an underrun; the outgoing audio is bridged smoothly while waiting for its next block. Reset the diagnostic counters to clear it.
WORK PEAKLargest combined time for rendering and the preceding Thru control/query calls.Compare it with RENDER PEAK to see the additional cost of interaction. It excludes work performed elsewhere in Move or Schwung.
WORK MISSESNumber of combined-work intervals that exceeded one audio-block period.Watch this as well as RENDER MISSES. A rise with no render misses indicates that control/query work contributed to overload. Zero cannot rule out an interruption elsewhere in the device.
LIMIT CURRENTGain reduction being applied by the final stereo safety limiter, in decibels.0.0 dB means no current limiting. Brief low values on peaks are normal; a sustained or large value means the summed level should be reduced.
LIMIT MAXLargest limiter reduction observed since takeover started.Use it as peak history. A large maximum with a near-zero current value describes an earlier transient, not continuous limiting.

Large counts are shortened with M or G suffixes so every value remains visible. The report is diagnostic history for the current takeover session; opening or closing it does not reset the counters. In any report view, press Shift+Delete to reset audio diagnostic peaks and miss counters for a fresh test. This report shortcut preserves samples, clips, and the running performance. One 128-frame block at 44.1 kHz lasts about 2,902 µs, and Move and Schwung also need part of that time. Peaks are displayed in whole microseconds; miss counters compare the original nanosecond timings with the block period, so display rounding cannot create a miss. The reports do not measure every cause of a missed device deadline.

Capture + Menu → Projects exports or imports a complete project archive with its referenced samples.

LED meanings

SurfaceState
PadsIn Single’s named Keys scales, playable pads are dark neutral grey and tonic pads are full white; the recalled map appears as soon as takeover opens. Active voices replace those colors with stable red, orange, yellow, green, azure, blue-violet, purple, and magenta slot colors. Split B uses muted teal and brighter teal roots; Shared Dual B uses muted plum and pink roots. Quad zones use neutral, teal, amber, and plum backgrounds with brighter roots. Active voice, cue, and roll colors take priority. Momentary notes return to their idle color on release. Latched notes remain lit until toggle-off or Hold Off expires; their voice color dims outside the stored pitch's pad mapping and brightens when that mapping returns.
StepsWhite occupied; orange active/stopped; green active/playing; red recording; blue-violet queued.
RecordRed during performance-data capture; orange during audio-lane capture; dim while idle.
PlayGreen while a performance loop runs; blue-violet while launch is queued.
Menu / BackDim when available; Menu bright in parameter/menu/help views; Back bright in secondary views.
UndoBright when a one-level safety undo is available; off when unavailable or recording. The key is the curved reverse arrow beside Shift.
Shift / DeleteDim when available, bright while held.
ArrowsDim in views where they navigate, bright while pressed; otherwise off.
Copy / Capture / MuteCopy bright in multi-instrument modes, dim in Single, off while recording blocks mode changes. Capture is dim in Single and A focus, bright for any non-A focus or the Pages hub. Mute is available only in multi-instrument modes and bright when its focused part is muted.
LoopBright white for Glitch Buffer Lock or a live Stack Hold; medium white for a recorded Stack Hold; dim white while scans run. The footer identifies the hold. In Quad, Loop is dark unless Glitch or Tape Inertia gives it a role.
TracksSelected lane on ordinary pages; selected clip bank in Clip Overview. While Capture or the Pages hub is active, they identify Voice, Lane, Mix/FX, and Performance.

In Bank mode, hold an empty right pad to record the SRC chosen on Bank Pads (IN, MIX, PT A or PT B). Release to finish; takes under 250 ms and effectively silent takes save nothing. Hold a filled pad to play, Shift + pad to latch, Delete (X) + pad to clear, and Undo to restore the assignment. A failed Bank take offers Retry / Discard from a main-encoder click on the waveform page.

In Layers, tap Capture to switch A/FX editing and restore each side’s last page. Hold an FX corner for about half a second or double tap it to toggle its editor. Editing remains open after release; repeat the gesture on the same corner or press Back to return. Shift + Back switches between the lane-and-FX overview and full-width waveform displays.

External MIDI

Musical notes, note-offs and polyphonic aftertouch use channels 1–4 for A–D according to the instrument mode. All Sound Off (CC 120) and All Notes Off (CC 123) are also accepted. External notes keep the pitches sent by the keyboard; Move’s pad scale does not quantize them.

High-resolution parameters use separate channels: 5–8 for A–D, 16 for shared controls. Part-control edits preserve Move’s selected part and page. Channels 5–8 and 16 also carry controller commands, so keep note input on 1–4. See MIDI control for setup, the current NRPN map and two-way feedback.

OXI E16 Remote

The Remote interface provides Engine, Stack, engine-specific and effect pages. Hold E16 Shift and turn encoder 1 to change pages. A occupies the top two encoder rows; B occupies the bottom two. The layout follows Single, Split, Shared Dual and Quad. Hold an encoder while turning for fine adjustment. In Quad, Shift + encoder 1 switches A/B ↔ C/D; Shift + encoder 16 leaves Remote. Edits preserve Move’s current part and page. See MIDI control for setup and connection checks.

High-resolution MIDI

The NRPN map has 364 fixed NRPN addresses. Channels 5–8 control A–D; channel 16 controls shared parameters. Musical notes and polyphonic aftertouch use channels 1–4. See the high-resolution MIDI guide for complete value messages, ranges and feedback.
