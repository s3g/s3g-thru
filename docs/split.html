Two instruments · one performance

Split mode

Divide the pad grid into two independent instruments. A uses the left four columns and stereo lanes 1–2; B uses the right four columns and stereo lanes 3–4. Each side has four source voices and its own engine, parameters, envelope, keyboard, stack movement, and insert effects.

Both sides may use the same engine. For example, put a slow Motion texture on A and a faster Motion rhythm on B, then blend between them. Single mode keeps the original four-lane, eight-voice instrument.

Start a Split performance

  1. Press Copy to enter Split. The first time, both sides inherit your current engine settings. Existing audio stays in its four lanes.
  2. Press Track 1 or 2 to select A and its recording/loading lane. Load or record material, then play the left half of the pads.
  3. Press Track 3 or 4 to select B. Load or record its material and play the right half.
  4. Press Capture (the broken-corner bracket button above Loop; button guide) to switch which side the parameter pages edit. Choose an engine and change its parameters. Playing the other side does not change editing focus.
  5. On the waveform screen, turn knob 8, BLND, to crossfade. Left is A, right is B. Hold Capture + turn knob 8 to reach Blend from any page.

Press Copy again to return to Single. Its engine and keyboard settings are remembered separately. Stop audio and performance recording before changing mode.

Editing and playing

The focused side has inverted lane numbers: 1–2 for A or 3–4 for B, drawn black on white. The A/B heading stays fixed. Parameter pages show A or B before the page name; shared pages show FX. Capture changes focus on release, and each side remembers its page. Track selection also selects the owning side. The main encoder keeps its existing navigation functions.

Both instruments continue to play as you edit either one. At either Blend endpoint the hidden instrument keeps its playheads and timing, so bringing it back reveals where it has reached. Mute fades the focused side out without stopping its playheads. The waveform header shows MUT in place of a muted part’s engine name, and the footer explains how to unmute it. Mute is remembered when you leave and return to Split. Sample preview follows that part’s mute, level and Blend. See Restore a silent part for a quick checklist.

Stack Start and scan paths cover the selected side’s two lanes. Their local positions 1 and 2 refer to physical lanes 1–2 on A and 3–4 on B. Cutups uses Lane and Advance within that same pair. Pressure and Loop Hold apply independently to each side; press Loop while the desired side is focused.

Each half has its own Pads & Scale and Amp Envelope settings. Shift + pad latches that side’s note. Slices use a 16-pad layout per side. Cues use the top four pads of each half, giving four cues per instrument. Rolls capture only their own side’s combined stereo playheads, before its inserts and Blend.

Pad colors: B’s idle pads use a dim teal tint; its scale roots use brighter teal. The startup footer briefly shows the installed version. This tint identifies the right-hand instrument even in Chromatic mode and appears as soon as Split is loaded. Active playheads, held-note brightness, stored cues, and rolls keep their usual status colors over the background tint. Unused rows in Cues remain off.

Waveform zoom

Each instrument’s two lanes show its own Position/Field window across the waveform width. POS moves that window and FLD changes its width; unused portions of the sample are cropped out. A and B zoom independently, so changing edit focus does not change either view. The regular four-lane instrument uses the same zoom across all four rows.

Playheads and outgoing readers use the same window as their waveform. If simultaneous voices use different regions, the view widens to include the sounding readers. With no notes active, it follows the instrument’s current window. Very narrow Fields enlarge the existing waveform overview, so less fine detail is available.

FX Sends

Hold Shift and press Capture to open FX Sends. All four sends and both wet return levels are on this page. A send feeds an effect; its return controls how much of that effect reaches the output. Delay and Reverb are shared stereo effects, with independent send amounts from A and B.

KnobDisplayRangeWhat it controls
1ADLY0–100%A Delay send. Increase this to hear A in the shared delay.
2AREV0–100%A Reverb send. Increase this to hear A in the shared reverb.
3BDLY0–100%B Delay send. Increase this to hear B in the shared delay.
4BREV0–100%B Reverb send. Increase this to hear B in the shared reverb.
5DRTN0–100%Delay Return level for both sides. Zero silences delay, even with a send raised. Start at 100% and use ADLY/BDLY to set the amount.
6RRTN0–100%Reverb Return level for both sides. Zero silences reverb. Start at 100% and use AREV/BREV to set the amount.
7CURVLinear / PowerBlend Curve. Power keeps a fuller midpoint with unrelated sources; Linear suits related or correlated material.
8BLND0–100%Blend moves from A alone at 0% to B alone at 100%.

Sends follow the instrument’s level, mute, and Blend. Existing echo and reverb tails continue after fading that side out. New Split setups start with DRTN and RRTN at 100%; recalled setups keep their saved levels. If a return is off, the footer identifies knob 5 or 6. Each part’s output level is LEVL on knob 8 of its Engine parameter page.

Hear an effect immediately: Shift + Capture → set knob 5 (DRTN) and knob 6 (RRTN) to 100% → play A and turn knob 1 (delay) or 2 (reverb). Play B and use knob 3 or 4. Keep Blend near the middle to hear both sides.

Find an effect page

From FX Sends, click the main encoder to open FX Pages. Turn to choose Delay, Reverb, the focused part’s Color or Stereo / Rotary inserts, shared Tape, or Effect Order. Click to open. Up/Down also selects a menu item. Back returns from a page to the menu, and Back again returns to FX Sends. Menu returns to the waveforms. Capture still switches the part being edited; the menu labels show A or B for private inserts.

To change a part’s dry level, focus it with Capture, open its Engine parameter page, and turn knob 8 (LEVL). Waveform knob 8 stays assigned to Blend.

Restore a silent part

  1. Use Capture to focus the silent side. Lane numbers 1–2 identify A; 3–4 identify B.
  2. If its header says MUT, press Mute. On Split Setup, MUTE should read Off. Leaving and returning to Split preserves mute.
  3. Set waveform knob 8, BLND, near the middle. Check LEVL on knob 8 of that part’s Engine page; 1.00 is a useful starting point.
  4. On Mix / Rec, check that its two lane gains are raised and its lanes contain audio. For browser audition, also raise knob 8, PREV.
  5. If only the effects are missing, open Shift + Capture. Raise the part’s send and the matching DRTN or RRTN return. These return levels do not silence dry notes.

While a clip plays, a live change to Blend, a part level, a send, or Curve overrides that parameter’s recorded movement until playback restarts or another clip launches. Keep a correction by recording it in an overdub. Browser preview follows the destination part’s level, mute, and Blend; see Preview routing.

Shift + Copy performs the same settings copy directly. After copying, edit the other side to vary its sound while keeping the same engine.

Part inserts and shared effects

Each side passes through its own Part Color page (Tone, Drive, Bits, Rate), followed by its own Stereo / Rotary page. These settings belong to that instrument. The insert order is fixed.

The two instruments then meet at Blend and feed the shared delay/reverb sends. Tape processes the combined stereo signal. FX Order lists Delay, Tape, and Reverb: move Tape before both returns, between them, or after both to choose which tails receive tape processing. Delay and Reverb use parallel sends; swapping only those two does not feed one into the other.

The full control descriptions remain in the Effects reference. DRTN and RRTN on FX Sends set the wet return levels. Return controls appear only there in Split; the corresponding cells on Delay and Reverb pages are reserved. The master compressor and limiter remain after the combined effects. If Tape Inertia is enabled, Loop’s transport gesture affects the shared Tape path.

Record, recall, and copy performances

Performance Record captures both sides together: notes already held when recording starts, subsequent notes and pressure, each side’s engine/inserts and stack holds, the shared sends, and Blend. Overdub adds a take. Finish recording with Record, then use Delete + Record to remove the newest take while the loop can keep playing. See Overdub undo. Clip Rate changes the speed of the combined performance.

Each clip stores its mode, both instrument setups, shared effect settings, and four source assignments. Clip selection and chaining recall that complete setup. Audio recording, input source, monitoring, tempo, and transport remain shared. Select a Track lane before capturing audio; newly recorded audio becomes that lane’s source as usual.

Mix over a playing clip

During loop playback, moving Blend, a part level, a send or Curve takes control of that parameter until you restart playback or launch another clip. This lets you restore an instrument even if recorded mixer automation had silenced it. Record the movement into an Overdub take when you want to keep it.

External MIDI

In Split, MIDI channel 1 addresses A and channel 2 addresses B. Other channels address A. Notes, note-offs, pressure, and instrument parameter CCs follow that channel without changing the visible editing focus. Shared controls apply to both instruments regardless of channel. See the complete MIDI map for CC 11–18: crossfade curve, Blend, levels, and sends.

Eight source voices remain the overall budget, divided four per side. Dense Weave or Wavesets settings and stereo effects still share Move’s processor. Check Shift + Menu’s render and work counters while testing your most demanding combination.
