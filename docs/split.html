Two instruments · one performance

Split and Shared Dual

Play two independent instruments from the pad grid: A on the left four columns, B on the right four. In Split, A uses stereo lanes 1–2 and B uses lanes 3–4. In Shared Dual, both instruments read the same four stereo lanes. Each side has four source voices and its own engine, parameters, envelope, keyboard, stack movement, and insert effects.

Both sides may use the same engine. For example, put a slow Motion texture on A and a faster Motion rhythm on B, then blend between them. Single mode keeps the original four-lane, eight-voice instrument.

Choose an instrument mode

Hold Capture and press Menu → choose Instrument Mode → click the main encoder. Turn or use Up/Down to choose Single, Split, Shared Dual, or Quad; click to apply. Back returns to Pages. Stop audio and performance recording before changing mode. Changing mode stops the current data loop and releases its notes.

Start a Split performance

  1. Choose Split from Instrument Mode. Afterward, Copy switches between Single and the last chosen dual mode. The first time, both sides inherit your current engine settings. Existing audio stays in its four lanes.
  2. Press Track 1 or 2 to select A and its recording/loading lane. Load or record material, then play the left half of the pads.
  3. Press Track 3 or 4 to select B. Load or record its material and play the right half.
  4. Press Capture (the broken-corner bracket button above Loop; button guide) to switch which side the parameter pages edit. Choose an engine and change its parameters. Playing the other side does not change editing focus.
  5. On the waveform screen, turn knob 8, BLND, to crossfade. Left is A, right is B. Hold Capture + turn knob 8 to reach Blend from any page.

Copy returns to Single and back to your last chosen dual mode. Single remembers its own engine and keyboard settings. Split and Shared Dual use the same A/B sound settings; changing the layout keeps those programs and maps Stack Start to the new lane range.

For four instruments with one lane and two voices each, see Quad mode.

Start a Shared Dual performance

  1. Choose Shared Dual from Instrument Mode.
  2. Load or record sources into Tracks 1–4. Both instruments can use each source; loading, editing, clearing, or recording a lane changes that shared source for both sides.
  3. Press Capture to focus A or B. Set its engine, Stack Start/Lane, keyboard, envelope, and private effects. Use the same engine with different settings, or choose two different engines.
  4. Play the left and right pad groups. Use waveform knob 8 (BLND), or Capture + knob 8 from another page, to crossfade.

Track buttons select the recording/loading/editing lane without changing A/B focus. Capture changes focus without changing that destination lane. Both sides can scan lanes 1–4; their paths, holds, pressure offsets, and lane gains remain independent. Mix / Rec provides all four lane gains for the focused instrument. Its recording controls remain shared.

Pad colors: Shared B uses muted plum for idle pads and brighter pink for scale roots. Split B remains teal. Active playhead, held-note, cue, and Roll colors show their usual status above this background. A keeps its existing colors.

Editing and playing

In Split, the focused side has inverted lane numbers: 1–2 for A or 3–4 for B, drawn black on white. The A/B heading stays fixed. Parameter pages show A or B before the page name; shared pages show FX. Capture changes focus on release, and each side remembers its page. In Split, Track selection also selects the owning side. Shared Dual keeps source selection separate from A/B focus. The main encoder keeps its existing navigation functions.

Both instruments continue to play as you edit either one. At either Blend endpoint the hidden instrument keeps its playheads and timing, so bringing it back reveals where it has reached. Mute fades the focused side out without stopping its playheads. The waveform header shows MUT in place of a muted part’s engine name, and the footer explains how to unmute it. Mute is remembered when you leave and return to Split. Part-mode sample preview follows the destination part’s mute, level, and Blend in Split; in Shared Dual it follows the currently focused part. Direct preview is independent; see Sample preview. See Restore a silent part for a quick checklist.

In Split, Stack Start and scan paths cover the selected side’s two lanes. Their local positions 1 and 2 refer to physical lanes 1–2 on A and 3–4 on B. Cutups uses Lane and Advance within that same pair. Shared Dual uses physical lane positions 1–4 for both instruments. Pressure and Loop Hold apply independently to each side; press Loop while the desired side is focused.

Each half has its own Pads & Scale and Amp Envelope settings. Shift + pad latches that side’s note. Slices use a 16-pad layout per side. Cues use the four pads in the bottom row of each half, giving four cues per instrument. Rolls capture only their own side’s combined stereo playheads, before its inserts and Blend.

Split pad colors: B’s idle pads use a dim teal tint; its scale roots use brighter teal. The startup footer briefly shows the installed version. This tint identifies the right-hand instrument even in Chromatic mode and appears as soon as Split is loaded. Active playheads, held-note brightness, stored cues, and rolls keep their usual status colors over the background tint. Unused rows in Cues remain off.

Waveform zoom

Each instrument’s two lanes show its own Position/Field window across the waveform width. POS moves that window and FLD changes its width; unused portions of the sample are cropped out. A and B zoom independently, so changing edit focus does not change either view. Single uses one zoom across all four rows. Shared Dual uses one four-row window controlled by the focused instrument’s POS and FLD, including while either or both sides play. Turn knob 1 to move the window and knob 2 to change its width. Capture switches the view to the other instrument’s settings. A wide Field on the other side, recorded readers, and release tails do not force the view to zoom out.

Playheads and outgoing readers use the same window as their waveform. In Single and Split, simultaneous voices with different regions widen the view to include sounding readers. Shared Dual keeps the focused edit window instead: readers outside it are hidden, while their audio continues. This also lets you adjust the view while a recorded performance plays; its stored parameter movement continues independently. Very narrow Fields enlarge the existing waveform overview, so less fine detail is available.

FX Sends

Hold Shift and press Capture to open FX Sends. All four sends and both wet return levels are on this page. A send feeds an effect; its return controls how much of that effect reaches the output. Delay and Reverb are shared stereo effects, with independent send amounts from A and B.

KnobDisplayRangeWhat it controls
1ADLY0–100%A Delay send. Increase this to hear A in the shared delay.
2AREV0–100%A Reverb send. Increase this to hear A in the shared reverb.
3BDLY0–100%B Delay send. Increase this to hear B in the shared delay.
4BREV0–100%B Reverb send. Increase this to hear B in the shared reverb.
5DRTN0–100%Delay Return level for both sides. Zero silences delay, even with a send raised. Start at 100% and use ADLY/BDLY to set the amount.
6RRTN0–100%Reverb Return level for both sides. Zero silences reverb. Start at 100% and use AREV/BREV to set the amount.
7CURVLinear / PowerBlend Curve. Power keeps a fuller midpoint with unrelated sources; Linear suits related or correlated material.
8BLND0–100%Blend moves from A alone at 0% to B alone at 100%.

Sends follow the instrument’s level, mute, and Blend. Existing echo and reverb tails continue after fading that side out. New dual setups start with DRTN and RRTN at 100%; recalled setups keep their saved levels. If a return is off, the footer identifies knob 5 or 6. Each part’s output level is LEVL on knob 8 of its Engine parameter page.

Hear an effect immediately: Shift + Capture → set knob 5 (DRTN) and knob 6 (RRTN) to 100% → play A and turn knob 1 (delay) or 2 (reverb). Play B and use knob 3 or 4. Keep Blend near the middle to hear both sides.

Find an effect page

From FX Sends, click the main encoder to open FX Pages. Turn to choose Delay, Reverb, the focused part’s Color or Stereo / Rotary inserts, shared Tape, or Effect Order. Click to open. Up/Down also selects a menu item. Back returns from a page to the menu, and Back again returns to FX Sends. Menu returns to the waveforms. Capture still switches the part being edited; the menu labels show A or B for private inserts.

To change a part’s dry level, focus it with Capture, open its Engine parameter page, and turn knob 8 (LEVL). Waveform knob 8 stays assigned to Blend.

A/B Mix Status

The waveform heading adds a source-voice count after each engine abbreviation and a thin audio-activity bar underneath. For a larger view, use Capture + Menu → A/B Mix Status. Meters follow the private inserts, part level, mute, and Blend; they exclude shared effect tails and sample preview. Counts include releasing source voices, up to four per part. Rolls can produce activity without increasing the source-voice count.

DisplayMeaning
ACTIVEA source voice or Roll is active; the meter shows its actual audio level.
MUTEDThe part’s Mute is on.
LEVEL 0The part’s Engine LEVL is zero.
FADED OUTBlend is at the opposite part’s endpoint.
NO AUDIOThe part’s two source lanes are empty in Split, or all four shared lanes are empty in Shared Dual.
IDLEThe part has source audio but no active voice or Roll.

Capture changes editing focus from this view. Menu or Back returns to the waveforms. The page is a readout; levels and sends retain their existing control locations.

Restore a silent part

  1. Use Capture to focus the silent side. Inverted lane numbers identify focus in Split; the inverted A/B header letter identifies it in Shared Dual.
  2. If its header says MUT, press Mute. On Dual Setup, MUTE should read Off. Leaving and returning to Split preserves mute.
  3. Set waveform knob 8, BLND, near the middle. Check LEVL on knob 8 of that part’s Engine page; 1.00 is a useful starting point.
  4. On Mix / Rec, check that its lane gains are raised (two in Split, four in Shared Dual) and its lanes contain audio. For browser audition, also raise knob 8, PREV.
  5. If only the effects are missing, open Shift + Capture. Raise the part’s send and the matching DRTN or RRTN return. These return levels do not silence dry notes.

While a clip plays, a live change to Blend, a part level, a send, or Curve overrides that parameter’s recorded movement until playback restarts or another clip launches. Keep a correction by recording it in an overdub. Part-mode browser preview follows the destination part in Split, or the focused part in Shared Dual, including its level, mute, and Blend; see Preview routing.

Dual Setup

KnobDisplayRangeWhat it controls
1MODESingle / Split / SharedMode selects Single, Split (two lanes per part), or Shared Dual (four lanes available to each part).
2EDITA / BEdit changes the side controlled by the parameter pages.
3COPYTurn to applyCopy duplicates the focused side’s engine, pad, envelope, lane-gain and insert settings to the other side. Samples, cues, recorded notes, the destination’s mute state, shared returns, and Blend stay in place.
4MUTEOff / OnMute fades out the focused side.

Shift + Copy performs the same settings copy directly. After copying, edit the other side to vary its sound while keeping the same engine.

Part inserts and shared effects

Each side passes through its own Part Color page (Tone, Drive, Bits, Rate), followed by its own Stereo / Rotary page. These settings belong to that instrument. The insert order is fixed.

The two instruments then meet at Blend and feed the shared delay/reverb sends. Tape processes the combined stereo signal. FX Order lists Delay, Tape, and Reverb: move Tape before both returns, between them, or after both to choose which tails receive tape processing. Delay and Reverb use parallel sends; swapping only those two does not feed one into the other.

The full control descriptions remain in the Effects reference. DRTN and RRTN on FX Sends set the wet return levels. Return controls appear only there in either dual mode; the corresponding cells on Delay and Reverb pages are reserved. The master compressor and limiter remain after the combined effects. If Tape Inertia is enabled, Loop’s transport gesture affects the shared Tape path.

Record, recall, and copy performances

Performance Record captures both sides together: notes already held when recording starts, subsequent notes and pressure, each side’s engine/inserts and stack holds, the shared sends, and Blend. Overdub adds a take. Finish recording with Record, then use Delete + Record to remove the newest take while the loop can keep playing. See Overdub undo. Clip Rate changes the speed of the combined performance.

Each clip stores its mode, both instrument setups, shared effect settings, and four source assignments. It also remembers the last chosen dual layout while Single is active. Existing Single/Split saves load normally. Before upgrading, export a backup of your set; sets saved by the current version require 0.28.0 or newer. Clip selection and chaining recall that complete setup. Audio recording, input source, monitoring, tempo, and transport remain shared. Select a Track lane before capturing audio; newly recorded audio becomes that lane’s source as usual.

Mix over a playing clip

During loop playback, moving Blend, a part level, a send or Curve takes control of that parameter until you restart playback or launch another clip. This lets you restore an instrument even if recorded mixer automation had silenced it. Record the movement into an Overdub take when you want to keep it.

External MIDI

In either dual mode, MIDI channel 1 addresses A and channel 2 addresses B. Other channels address A. Notes, note-offs, pressure, and instrument parameter CCs follow that channel without changing the visible editing focus. Shared controls apply to both instruments regardless of channel. See the complete MIDI map for CC 11–18: crossfade curve, Blend, levels, and sends.

Eight source voices remain the overall budget, divided four per side. Dense Weave or Wavesets settings and stereo effects still share Move’s processor. Check Shift + Menu’s render and work counters while testing your most demanding combination.
